2.      Why is it 'Angry Packers Fan' and not 'Angry Packer Fan'?

     Good question. I used to refer to myself as a 'Packer Fan' or a 'Brewer Fan' or a 'Badger Fan' until a friend of mine--let's just say she's an English whiz--pointed out to me that as far as grammar goes, it was wrong, it should be 'Packers Fan' or 'Brewers Fan'. Usually I give a rat's ass about proper grammar, but once she explained it to me, I had to admit she was right.

     I can't really remember all the details-I've drank a shit-ton of High Life since then, so my memory is hazy-but it went something like this: The sentences "I'm a fan of the Green Bay Packers" and "I'm a Green Bay Packers Fan" basically mean the same thing, right? Ok then, since we already know the Packers are in Green Bay, so we can write these two as "I'm a fan of the Packers" and "I'm a Packers Fan" and again the two sentences still mean the same thing, right? Well, now replace 'Packers' in both sentences with 'Packer' and you get: "I'm a Packer fan" and "I'm a fan of the Packer". You see? Saying you're a Packer fan is the same thing as saying you're a fan of a single Packer, which of course you likely are, but if you're trying to say you're a fan of the Green Bay Packers, you gotta say "I'm a Packers Fan". I'm a Packers fan, and I'm angry, hence Angry Packers Fan. Got it?
